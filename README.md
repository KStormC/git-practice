## Why is this article intresting?
This article stood out to me because it raises an important point about ownership and agency, especially in the age of generative AI. Section 230 seems to clearly protect tech companies against what their users post--proponents suggest it's required to protect free speech on the internet whereas its opponents believe that it offers too much protection and doesn not incenvitize big tech to reform their discrminatory practices, if any.

This, to me, raises the important question of how do we determine how much control a specific platform has in shaping or distributing user generated content. LLM's can be used to investigate this question further: since their outputs combine user input, web searches, training data and company guidelines, it would be quite difficult to determine who owns the produced knowledge and who is to be held liable(user, company, someone else?) in the event of a lawsuit. One solution to this problem may be to establish some set of guidleines that regulate how AI models learn and provide output which would help define what may constitute as punishable behaviour under Section 230. The broader question that lies here really is how exactly do we determine how much contribution (by AI to develop/improve both existing and new content) is too much? The ultimate goal should be to promote continued innovation while being mindful of the legal ramifications of what the innovation produces.
